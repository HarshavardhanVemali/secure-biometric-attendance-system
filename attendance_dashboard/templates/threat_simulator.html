"Why do we even need the Raspberry Pi?"
If you remove the Raspberry Pi and plug the domain directly into the biometric device, you are forced to disable almost all the security architecture. Here is exactly how an attacker breaches the system:
⚠️
Directory Brute-Forcing
An attacker doesn't need physical access. Using common hacker tools like DirBuster or Gobuster, they shoot 10,000 guesses per second at your domain. Within 45 seconds, the tool successfully discovers your unprotected /api/v1/gateway/sync/ endpoint.
💀
The Loss of Cryptography
Biometric scanners are highly vulnerable and only send raw text (e.g., User 101 punched in). The Pi acts as a Cryptographic Vault, wrapping raw data in AES-256 and Anti-Replay Nonces. Without it, the hacker can sit at a coffee shop and forge a raw HTTP POST request to mark themselves present without ever touching the scanner.
🦹‍♂️
Network Sniffing (MITM)
Older scanners do not process modern strict HTTPS; they silently drop down to unencrypted HTTP. If an attacker jumps on the same LAN or Wi-Fi, they can use Wireshark to sniff the air. They will instantly read your Sync URL, Employee IDs, and Times in raw plain-text.
🏆
CONCLUSION: THE ARMORED TRUCK
The Raspberry Pi is the unsung hero of the security architecture. You keep the vulnerable biometric scanner physically wired exclusively to the Pi, meaning the scanner's raw vulnerabilities never touch the internet. The Pi acts as an impenetrable armored truck that safely transports the data to the cloud.
